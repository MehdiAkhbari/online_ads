HYPERPARAMETER CHOICES FOR THE CAUSAL FOREST (adsim.estimate)
================================================================

This documents where min_samples_split, min_samples_leaf, max_samples,
max_depth, and n_estimators (CausalForestDML, fit in
adsim.estimate.fit_one_rank) come from, and how they were chosen. These
are no longer cross-validated the way param_grid (the M/E nuisance
model grid) is -- they are derived directly from the outcome's click
rate, because the statistical constraint that matters (enough clicks
per leaf to estimate anything) is known in advance and isn't something
grid-search comparison is well suited to discover.

THE CORE CONSTRAINT: A ~0.1% CLICK RATE
----------------------------------------
is_clicked averages about 0.1% across the data. A tree leaf of size N
has an expected click count of roughly 0.001 * N, and (treating clicks
as a rare Bernoulli event) the leaf's own click-rate estimate has
relative standard error of about 1 / sqrt(0.001 * N):

    leaf size    expected clicks    relative SE of leaf's rate
    ---------    ---------------    --------------------------
        2,000            2                    ~71%
        5,000            5                    ~45%
       10,000           10                    ~32%
       20,000           20                    ~22%
       50,000           50                    ~14%
      100,000          100                    ~10%

"A few clicks" (5-20) still means 20-45% relative noise per leaf --
enough to detect large heterogeneity, not fine distinctions. ~10%
precision needs leaves near 100K, which is at or beyond the *entire*
combined sample size for several of the rarer advertiser ranks (every
per-rank fit combines the base ad, ~100K rows, with that rank's own
rows, which range from ~150 to ~2.6M). That's a real ceiling set by
the data, not something any hyperparameter choice can lift -- picking a
target leaf size is picking a point on this precision-vs-heterogeneity
tradeoff, not eliminating it.

min_samples_leaf: 10,000
-------------------------
This is the parameter that actually floors leaf size. It was ABSENT
from the tuning grid before this change (econml default: 5), which
means leaves as small as 5 rows were possible regardless of what
min_samples_split was set to -- min_samples_split only gates whether a
node is *eligible* to split, it does not bound how small the resulting
children can be. At a 0.1% click rate, a 5-row leaf has an expected
0.005 clicks: pure noise, not signal. 10,000 corresponds to ~10
expected clicks per leaf, "a few clicks" per the target above.

min_samples_split: 20,000
---------------------------
Set to ~2x min_samples_leaf, which is the natural relationship: a node
can't be split unless BOTH children can meet the leaf floor, so
min_samples_split must be at least 2x min_samples_leaf for a split to
even be possible. This value is no longer grid-searched (previously
[1000, 2000, 5000]) -- see "why these aren't tuned" below.

max_samples: 50,000 (absolute count, not a fraction)
-------------------------------------------------------
This is the single most important mechanical change. It used to be
0.01 -- a FRACTION of each rank's own combined sample size. Because
combined N varies enormously across ranks (~100K for the rarest ads up
to ~2.7M for the most common, since every fit includes the ~100K-row
base ad plus that rank's own rows), a fixed fraction gives wildly
different absolute per-tree sample sizes: ~1,000 rows/tree for the
smallest ranks, ~27,000 for the largest. That's backwards for a
leaf-size requirement that's the same everywhere (it depends on the
click rate, not on how popular the ad is) -- the old 0.01 fraction gave
the SMALLEST ranks the FEWEST rows per tree, right when they can least
afford it: with min_samples_split=1000 (old) and a ~1000-row-per-tree
budget, and with min_samples_leaf defaulting to 5, trees for the
smallest ranks were already right at the edge of not being able to
split at all.

Switching to an absolute integer (50,000) fixes the per-tree sample
size regardless of rank. econml's max_samples supports this directly
("if int, train each tree on max_samples samples"). 50,000 leaves
comfortable room above the 20,000/10,000 split/leaf floors for a
couple of splits per tree, even after honest-forest sample splitting
(honest=True internally divides each tree's subsample into a
splitting half and an estimation half). For the smallest ranks
(~100K total) this means each tree sees roughly half the data -- bagging
diversity across trees is more limited there than for the large ranks,
but that's an honest consequence of needing large-enough leaves given
how rare clicks are, not a hyperparameter that can be tuned away.

max_depth: 10 (fixed, no longer a 3-value grid)
--------------------------------------------------
With min_samples_split/min_samples_leaf this large relative to
max_samples, min_samples_split becomes the practical stopping rule long
before depth becomes relevant: log2(50000 / 20000) ~= 1.3, i.e. trees
are expected to manage only one or two splits before hitting the split
floor, regardless of whether max_depth is 10, 20, or 30. The previous
[10, 20, 30] grid was very likely producing three structurally
identical trees per candidate for most ranks -- spending a third of
cf_param_grid's search time for no real difference in the result. 10
is kept as a generous safety cap, not an active constraint.

n_estimators: 500 (production default; use a smaller value for quick
tests, e.g. --n-estimators 100)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
More trees reduce the variance of the forest's aggregated CATE
estimate and matter directly for BLB-based inference (the confidence
intervals produced by cf.fit(..., inference="blb", ...)) approximating
its asymptotic distribution well. 100-300 (the old, partly accidental,
value -- see below) is on the low side for that; 500-1000 is more
typical in the causal-forest literature for estimates meant to be
reported with confidence intervals. Because min_samples_split/leaf are
now much larger, each individual tree is cheaper to build (far fewer
candidate splits to search before hitting the floor), which is roughly
what pays for using more trees -- see the runtime comparison recorded
separately for actual measured numbers, this is not just a theoretical
offset.

WHY min_samples_split/leaf/max_samples/max_depth ARE NO LONGER IN
cf_param_grid (compared via cross-validation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
param_grid (the separate grid used for the E/M nuisance models, still
grid-searched as before) makes sense to cross-validate: which
max_depth/min_samples_split combination best predicts Y or T is
genuinely a question CV can answer, since it's an ordinary prediction
problem on the full sample (not click-rate-constrained the same way).
But for the causal forest's own min_samples_leaf/min_samples_split/
max_samples/max_depth, the deciding constraint (enough clicks per leaf
to say anything) is known in advance from the click rate, and CV
comparison across candidates doesn't have any way to "discover" a
statistical requirement like that better than just computing it. Worse,
letting these vary in cf_param_grid re-introduces the mechanical
problem below.

A REAL BUG THIS CHANGE ALSO FIXES: cf.tune() overriding the final
model's hyperparameters
-------------------------------------------------------------------
econml's CausalForestDML.tune() (see the source, causal_forest.py) does
something easy to miss: after finding the best combination from
cf_param_grid, it writes EVERY key in that dict back onto the original
estimator object -- not a copy -- via `setattr(self, key, value)`. The
grid previously had 'n_estimators': [300], so after tune() ran, the
estimator's own n_estimators was left at 300 regardless of what the
CausalForestDML(..., n_estimators=100, ...) constructor call said --
the constructor's 100 was silently overwritten by tune()'s 300 before
the subsequent cf.fit(...) call, meaning production forests were
actually being built with 300 trees, not the 100 the code visually
suggested. Removing 'n_estimators' (and now max_depth,
min_samples_split, min_samples_leaf) from cf_param_grid means tune()
never touches them, so whatever fit_one_rank's CausalForestDML(...)
call sets is what actually gets used for the final fit -- no more
silent override.

CLI OVERRIDES
--------------
adsim.estimate now accepts --n-estimators, --min-samples-split,
--min-samples-leaf, --max-samples, --max-depth (defaults as above), so
these can be tested at a smaller/cheaper n_estimators before committing
to the full production value without any code changes.
